# HOW TO CHOOSE QUESTIONS (do this before every post)
1. Start from the customer, not the keyword. Ask: what would a busy owner or office manager type into Google, or ask ChatGPT, the week before they'd hire us?
2. Mine real questions from: Google "People Also Ask" and related searches, Reddit (r/smallbusiness, r/PropertyManagement, r/ecommerce, r/Entrepreneur, and industry subreddits), Quora, YouTube comments on competitor videos, 2–3 star reviews of automation agencies and freelancers, and industry Facebook and LinkedIn groups.
3. Check the current top 5 results for the target query. Identify the search intent (informational, commercial, transactional, or local) and the format Google rewards (list, guide, comparison table, calculator, or definition).
4. Find the gap: what do the top results get wrong, skip, leave vague, or fail to update? Most competitors are vague on price and results; we aren't. If you can't find a gap, pick a different topic.
5. Map "fan-out" sub-questions: the 5–10 follow-up questions someone would ask next. Answer them in the post as H2s and H3s; this is how AI Overviews select sources.
6. Reject the topic if it duplicates an existing post or page on our site (cannibalization; check the sitemap first), has no connection to what we sell, can only be answered with generic advice, or can only be answered by naming tools or explaining how automations are built.

# SOURCING STANDARDS
Use sources in this priority order:
- Tier 1: Primary data — government sites (.gov), official statistics (BLS, Census, SBA, the Federal Reserve's small business surveys, and similar), and peer-reviewed research.
- Tier 2: Reputable industry research — established trade associations (for example NARPM, the National Apartment Association, and the NFIB), recognized analyst firms, and major publications' original reporting.
- Tier 3: Expert commentary — named practitioners with verifiable credentials.
- Avoid: software vendors' marketing, other SEO blogs and content farms, undated statistics, "studies show" with no study, stats that only appear in listicles, and any source more than 3 years old unless it's the most recent available (say so if it is).

Rules:
- Every statistic must link to the original source, not a site that re-quoted it. Trace it back.
- Include the year of every statistic in the sentence ("a 2025 BLS survey found...").
- If you cannot verify a claim, cut it. Never invent statistics, quotes, studies, tools, pricing, or customer stories.
- Minimum 3 Tier 1–2 sources per post; cite them inline with descriptive anchor text.
- Flag any claim about pricing, laws, or regulations as [VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISHING] if it could have changed recently.

# WRITING THE POST
- Answer the main question directly in the first 2–3 sentences (40–60 words). This is the passage Google and AI tools extract. No throat-clearing intros.
- Follow with a short "Key takeaways" box (3–5 bullets).
- Use question-based H2s that mirror how people actually phrase searches.
- Each section opens with a direct one-sentence answer, then expands.
- Use tables for comparisons, numbered steps for decisions and checklists, and specifics over adjectives ("cut a lease approval from up to 2 hours to about 10 minutes," not "dramatically faster").
- Reading level: grade 8–9. Short paragraphs. Active voice. Write like a practical operations advisor talking to a busy owner, never salesy. "We" is KeyProcess Automation Services and "you" is the reader.
- Banned: "In today's fast-paced world," "Let's dive in," "It's important to note," "game-changer," "unlock," "leverage," "elevate," "navigate the landscape," "streamline your workflow," "seamless," and any filler that could appear in any post on any site.
- Length is whatever it takes to fully answer the query better than the current #1 result — usually 1,200–2,000 words for supporting posts. Never pad.
- End with an FAQ section (4–6 real questions from PAA and forums, 2–4 sentence answers) and a CTA tied to the specific problem the post solved, pointing to the free audit at /audit. Where it fits, mention the 30-day guarantee or the savings calculator at /#calculator.

# ON-PAGE SEO CHECKLIST
- Primary keyword in: title tag, H1, first 100 words, URL slug, meta description, and at least one H2. Use naturally — never stuff.
- Title tag: under 60 characters before " | KeyProcess Automation Services," includes the primary keyword and a reason to click (year, number, or specific outcome).
- Meta description: 140–155 characters, states the answer or benefit, ends with an implicit CTA.
- URL slug: short, lowercase, hyphenated, no dates or stop words. Posts live at /blog/<slug>.
- Internal links: 3–5 to relevant existing pages (/results or a specific case, /#calculator, /audit, the industry pages, and earlier posts) plus 1 link to the cluster pillar; descriptive anchor text, never "click here." Also suggest 2 existing pages that should link TO this new post.
- External links: to Tier 1–2 sources only.
- Images: one branded featured image (1200x630, title plus short category label) made with our own template; suggest 1–2 original diagrams or checklists if they help. No stock photos and no screenshots of tools.
- Schema: provide JSON-LD for BlogPosting (with author and dateModified), BreadcrumbList, and FAQPage; add HowTo where it fits.
- Author byline and short bio. Until an author page exists, link the bio to /results.
