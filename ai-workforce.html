Approvals and guardrails

"Will it do things I didn't OK?" No.

It's the first question every owner asks, so these rules are built in from day one.

  • Nothing customer-facing goes out without approval. Emails, social posts, ads, promotions, and public website copy all wait for your yes.
  • Ad spend is owner-only. No desk changes a budget, and only the named owner can approve a spend change.
  • Only named people can approve. A casual "sounds cool" is treated as input. Only a clear yes to a clear ask counts.
  • Delegated authority is explicit. You can let a partner or manager approve certain work, and everything they approve is listed for you to review.
  • Backups before every change. Every page or site edit is backed up first, so anything can be rolled back.
  • Your business rules are written into each desk. For example: never link to competitors, never invent reviews or customer quotes, and check what's already scheduled before planning more.
  • Decisions stick. When you decline something or delete a plan, it stays gone. Desks don't quietly bring it back.
  • People decisions stay human. Hiring, firing, medical, legal, and financial calls are made by people.
  • Private data stays private. Scorecards show order numbers and totals, never customer names, emails, or addresses. Logins stay in your own accounts.

Case study: proven first in marketing

A DTC beverage brand's first five days with an AI workforce

A small direct-to-consumer beverage company with three product lines, two websites, an email list of about 5,000, paid ads, and daily social posts. Two co-owners ran it with no marketing staff. One owner handled all the marketing, spend was judged by feel, and about a third of orders couldn't be traced to any channel.

We built a marketing office of 13 AI desks plus two owner desks, covering email, social, ads, website and SEO, creative, copywriting, a lead for each product line, retention, attribution, and a general manager. We connected 16 of the business's systems, and the co-owner sends ideas and approves work from a private team chat channel.

The results line up with the three things an AI workforce is for: seeing what's really going on, getting more done, and adding to the bottom line.

FAQ

AI Workforce questions

What is an AI workforce?

An AI workforce is a team of AI employees, each with one job. At KeyProcess Automation Services, every AI employee has a desk, a name, a written job description, a task list, connections to the tools its job needs, and a scorecard that shows what it delivered and what it cost. You hand out work the way you would to a person, and you approve anything that matters.

Will the AI employees do things I didn't approve?

No. Nothing customer-facing goes out and no money is spent without a clear yes from a named person. Ad spend is owner-only, every change is backed up first, and when you decline something it stays declined.

Which departments can have an AI workforce?

Any department with repeatable work: sales, customer service, operations, finance and admin, HR and recruiting, marketing, and leadership. It was proven first in marketing, and the same desks, approvals, and scorecards fit every other department.

What tools can the AI employees work in?

If your team logs into it, your AI employees can probably work in it: your CRM, inbox, calendar, files, spreadsheets, accounting, online store, help desk, project boards, team chat, website, and ad accounts. Your logins stay in your own accounts.

How long does it take to set up?

About four weeks. Week 1 is discovery and connecting your tools, week 2 is building the office, and weeks 3 and 4 are a pilot that ends with a before-and-after report. After that, a monthly plan keeps the workforce running and improving.

Does an AI workforce replace my staff?

It takes repeatable work off your team so people spend their time deciding instead of doing. Hiring, firing, medical, legal, and financial decisions always stay with people.
